## 2. Precedence model (which document governs what)

When documents disagree, the higher rank wins for the type of decision it governs.

| Rank | Source | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The owner's latest explicit instruction in chat | Everything. The owner is the final product owner. |
| 2 | `AGENTS.md` | Brand, positioning facts, descriptor, services, entity and SEO/AEO/GEO rules, URL and content-model principles, engineering quality gates (section 80A), prohibited behavior |
| 3 | `DECHY_CONTENT_MASTER.md` | Content depth, page composition, homepage and service blueprints, visual content standard, content QA, definition of done |
| 4 | `DECHY_CLAUDE_MASTER_HANDOFF.md` | Claude's role, audit and approval gates, working principles |
| 5 | `docs/planning/*` (approved Phase 0 plan) | Concrete architecture, designs, and decisions the owner approved |
| 6 | `docs/decisions/*` (decision records) | Why an approved amendment was made; the amendment ledger (ADR 0002) |
| 7 | Implementation details | Code conventions and process notes, including this file's process sections |

Rules for conflicts:
1. **Owner-approved amendments are marked in place.** AGENTS.md, the Content Master, and the Handoff carry `AMENDED`, `SUPERSEDED`, `CLARIFIED`, or `EXTENDED` notes citing an amendment number (AMD-nn). The full ledger is `docs/decisions/0002-governance-reconciliation.md`. Where a note exists, follow the amendment, not the older text beneath it. Everything unannotated stays in force as written.
2. If you find a conflict not covered by an amendment, do not guess and do not follow the older text silently: state the conflict, recommend a resolution, and ask.
3. If this file ever appears to contradict a higher-ranked source, the higher-ranked source wins; fix this file.

## 4. Non-negotiable rules (summaries; sources prevail)

- Official descriptor, exact: **Technology, Software, AI & Digital Solutions**. Never shorten or replace it. Rejected tagline: "Technology. People. Progress." Never use it.
- Company statement (approved; do not alter the figures): "Since 2010, DECHY has helped over 1,500 organizations, companies, firms, brands, businesses, individuals, professionals, teams, and other clients across a wide range of industries and business types."
- DECHY is a global company. No physical offices, addresses, branches, local teams, or Google Business Profiles; no city pages. Country pages mean "DECHY serves clients in this market" and are international search and content expansion, not exclusive priority markets.
- All 15 service pillars stay significant and visible: Software Development; Web Development; Mobile App Development; AI & Machine Learning; Automation, Workflows & AI Solutions; Cloud & DevOps; Cybersecurity; Data & Analytics; API & Systems Integration; Ecommerce Solutions; UI/UX & Product Design; Branding & Creative; SEO, AEO & GEO; Digital Marketing; Consulting & Digital Transformation. Navigation families only organize them; none is removed, merged, hidden, downgraded, or discredited. AI, Automation, Web, or SEO never become the whole identity or outrank other services; emphasis is made by structure, not ranking language.
- The full capability architecture is approved and stays in the entity graph. Internal classification organizes; it never appears publicly as a disclaimer and never reduces positioning. Describe capabilities with legitimate generalized descriptions. Specific evidence (clients, projects, people, locations, metrics, testimonials, awards, certifications, case-study outcomes, dates) appears only when actually known or supplied. Illustrative examples are labeled illustrative.
- Four axes stay distinct: Services are what DECHY does, Work Areas are the kinds of system it builds, Solutions are the business problem or outcome, Technologies are what it can be built with. A Solution earns a page only when its intent differs from its closest Work Area (ADR 0006). Grouped technologies (`partOf`) never imply a page.
- Entity does not automatically mean page. Each concept has one canonical home service. Service-by-technology pages are canonical for "X development" intent. Problems open Solution pages. Never create thin pages to raise URL count.
- Unbuilt references render as plain text, never dead links, fake routes, "Coming Soon", or placeholder pages.
- Work is a substantial primary section with two layers: Work Areas (substantive capability pages) and Work Entries (real projects from supplied facts only). A case study is a verified, detailed Work Entry.
- Style: no em dashes, no en dashes, no emojis in DECHY content or docs. Use "to" for ranges.
- Brand tokens are fixed: #111827, #2F6FED, #F7F6F2, #E5E7EB, #6B7280; Manrope headings, Source Sans 3 body, monospace only where technically useful. Use the supplied PNG logo as provided; never redraw, recolor, stretch, or regenerate it. The site logo is the wordmark without the descriptor; the descriptor version is the social sharing image only (owner, 7 October 2026).
- Typography follows the measured system, AMENDED by the owner on 7 October 2026: normal body 16 to 17px (never below 16px), reading text 17 to 18px, lead 18 to 20px, statements 19 to 22px, H1 32 to 52px, captions and labels 14px, buttons and inputs 16px, nothing important below 14px, measures 62ch (articles) and 64ch (service), touch targets at least 44px. Never fall back to tiny text.
- Clarity and comprehension (permanent, ADR 0004): a normal visitor must be able to understand what DECHY does without being technical. Pages move the visitor from problem to understanding to options to solution to next step. Use plain, direct language and explain any necessary technical term the first time it appears. Explain depth in layers (plain meaning, why it matters, how DECHY approaches it, then technical detail in expandable panels that stay in the page). Never make information available only on hover. Every diagram teaches one stated thing (purpose, takeaway, plain labels, a caption that says what it shows) and agrees with its heading and copy. Keep search language and technical accuracy while being clear. The site must feel like a solution system with obvious pathways (custom software, app, automation, AI, connect systems, cloud, security, online store, search visibility, help deciding). Before a page is complete, answer from the page alone: what does DECHY do here, what problem does it solve, who is it for, what would you choose, what next. Never make the site technically impressive at the expense of being understandable.
- Content voice (owner direction, 6 October 2026): sound like an experienced technology company explaining a solution to a prospective client, not a textbook, a lesson, a glossary, or an SEO article. Order: what it means, why it matters, where it fits, options, what DECHY can do, technical depth where useful, next step. A short definition is fine when it aids understanding; never add one only for SEO. Generalized examples use natural wording ("a typical system might", "a business could") and are never presented as DECHY client work; do not repeat disclaimer boxes. Technology pages are never thin and never renamed copies. No technology-coverage validator; no llms.txt.
- Visual quality (permanent, ADR 0003): every visual lives in a frame that owns its width, with its caption inside it; diagrams are grid-based HTML with real type (never scaled vector text), laid out by their own container width, with supporting items in the same rows as what they support; photographs are sharp, high-resolution (source at least 2400 px), well composed, consistent, free of clichés, and are inspected at 100 percent and at rendered size on 1x and 2x before use; people are never chosen by demographic; no image source or credit is ever visible on the site (provenance only in the registry and license evidence; do not use assets that require visible attribution). Inspect alignment, spacing, grid, captions, wrapping and balance on every new page before calling it done.
- Responsive design: mobile, tablet, laptop, desktop, and large desktop are all first-class; validate at 320, 360, 375, 390, 430, 768, 820, 1024, 1280, 1440, 1920, and 2560 px and check navigation, typography, spacing, grids, buttons, forms, images, diagrams, content width, stacking, cropping, density, overflow, and touch targets.
- Company facts live only in the Approved Facts Register (created in Phase 1 under `data/facts/`). External technical research stays in the research log and never becomes a company fact or weakens a capability claim.

## 5. Approval required before action

Present, do not decide silently, major changes to: brand; business positioning; core service architecture; domain strategy; major information architecture or URL rules; major technical architecture or stack; public factual claims; scope. Also required: Netlify account actions, DNS or domain changes, and publishing anything outside the repository other than a normal Git push.

Normal implementation decisions (component structure, libraries within the approved stack, copy refinement, image selection and download under the visual rules, test design) are the agent's to make; no per-image or per-batch approval is needed. Claude still challenges weak decisions and reports risks.

## 6. Working method, Git, and deployment

- Method: plan, implement, test, inspect (in a real browser for UI, at phone, tablet, and desktop widths and at every container-query switch), diagnose and fix, re-verify, commit, push. Run the checklist in `docs/design/layout-and-visual-quality-rules.md` before calling any page complete. Follow `AGENTS.md` section 80A. Do not present knowingly broken work; report honestly what was and was not verified. Do not ask the owner to do work Claude can do; the owner never manually downloads or uploads project files.
- Flow: local implementation, tests, browser inspection, fixes, commit, push to GitHub, then Netlify deployment or preview.
- Verify `git remote -v` shows `Samogunde/DECHY-SITE` before any push. The repository stays private. Never rewrite or force-push existing commits.
- Commit identity for this repository: `Samogunde <102127730+Samogunde@users.noreply.github.com>` (set in the local repo config; verify with `git config user.email` before committing).
- Commits end with the co-author line required by the session attribution instructions. Conventional prefixes: `feat:`, `fix:`, `content:`, `docs:`, `chore:`. Never commit secrets. Keep binary masters out of Git unless explicitly approved.
- Production code: short-lived branches, CI green, then merge to `main`; never knowingly push broken production work.
- Netlify: authorization is not required for foundation work. When a step truly requires it, stop at that specific dependency and tell the owner exactly what is needed.
- Contact form: securely configurable destination, no invented inbox, no credentials in the repository.
