# DECHY CONTENT MASTER

> **GOVERNANCE NOTE (2026-10-04).** Annotated in place by `docs/decisions/0002-governance-reconciliation.md`. Original wording preserved; follow cited amendments where marked. Precedence and the source-of-truth map are in `CLAUDE.md`.

## 1. Purpose

This document is the companion content-production specification for `AGENTS.md`.
`AGENTS.md` remains the engineering, architecture, brand, SEO, AEO, GEO, and governance authority. This document defines the content depth, page composition, content ownership, visual requirements, and editorial standards that the coding/content agent must follow.

DECHY is not to be implemented as a thin corporate brochure. It is a substantial global technology company website representing:

**DECHY — Technology, Software, AI & Digital Solutions**

The website must communicate breadth, technical depth, credibility, practical usefulness, and commercial relevance.

---

## 2. Content Ownership Model

Content is produced collaboratively in two layers.

### Layer A — Strategic source content

The master planner provides:

- canonical DECHY positioning
- approved company facts and claims
- page purpose and search intent
- required topics and entities
- required sections
- content relationships
- SEO targets and semantic coverage
- AEO question targets
- GEO/entity relationships
- internal-link targets
- visual/content requirements
- CTA direction
- content depth expectations

### Layer B — Implementation content

The coding/content agent is responsible for:

- drafting complete page copy from the approved blueprint
- expanding sections with useful, specific explanations
- adapting copy to the page's actual entity and intent
- writing headings, supporting copy, lists, tables, FAQs, and CTAs
- creating structured content records
- connecting content to entities and internal links
- implementing metadata and schema
- proposing appropriate visuals

The coding/content agent must not invent DECHY facts, clients, results, offices, employees, testimonials, certifications, partnerships, statistics, or proof.

When a claim is not supported by the master specification or supplied evidence, omit the claim or flag it for review.

### Review rule

The master planner and user review the first representative pages before large-scale content expansion. Once the content standard is approved, the agent may apply it consistently to qualified pages while continuing to follow the same QA rules.

---

## 3. Non-Negotiable Content Standard

DECHY pages must be substantial enough to satisfy a real visitor before they are considered complete.

Do not measure quality by word count alone. Measure it by coverage, usefulness, specificity, clarity, evidence, entity relationships, and ability to answer the user's next questions.

A page must not feel empty because:

- the body copy is tiny
- sections contain one or two sentences
- cards replace meaningful explanations
- large blank areas substitute for content
- every section repeats the same idea
- the same generic template is populated with different keywords

Avoid artificial padding. Expand by adding genuinely useful information.

## 4. Content Depth Model

Every major entity supports:

### Micro

1–2 concise sentences for navigation, cards, relationship lists, and compact UI.

### Short

A useful summary for category pages, cards, directories, and metadata-supporting contexts.

### Medium

A meaningful overview explaining what the entity is, who it serves, what it covers, and why it matters.

### Long

A detailed explanation covering capabilities, applications, relationships, implementation considerations, and relevant business context.

### Full page content

A complete commercial or informational page with enough depth to answer the primary intent, secondary questions, related entities, objections, implementation concerns, and next-step considerations.

The homepage and major service pages require full-page treatment.

---

## 5. SEO, AEO, and GEO Content Standard

Content must serve three related discovery systems without writing unnaturally for algorithms.

### SEO

Each page must have:

- one clear primary search intent
- one primary topic/entity
- meaningful secondary topics
- semantic entities
- natural keyword coverage
- descriptive headings
- useful internal links
- relevant supporting content
- unique title and meta description
- appropriate structured data
- crawlable, indexable content where qualified

### AEO

Important questions must receive direct, clear answers.

Use:

- concise definitions
- direct answer paragraphs
- comparison tables when useful
- step-by-step explanations
- question-based sections
- FAQs where genuinely useful
- explicit terminology
- examples and practical guidance

Do not create FAQ blocks solely to add keywords.

### GEO / AI search

Pages must make relationships explicit and understandable.

Connect:

**DECHY → service → technology → solution → problem → industry → business type → integration → use case → evidence/content**

Use clear entity names, factual descriptions, consistent terminology, related entities, technical explanations, original guidance, and publicly understandable relationships.

Do not claim that content will be cited or recommended by any AI system.

## 10. Visual Content Standard

> **CLARIFIED (AMD-04).** Image sourcing proceeds under the standing authorization in ADR 0002 section 2 item 1: license and provenance recorded, no per-image owner approval, nothing fabricated as evidence.

The website must not depend entirely on text and cards.

Use high-quality visuals wherever they improve understanding, hierarchy, credibility, or conversion.

Preferred visual types:

- authentic project screenshots when available and permitted
- real product/interface imagery when legitimately sourced
- licensed professional photography
- technical architecture diagrams
- workflow diagrams
- process diagrams
- data visualizations
- product/system relationship diagrams
- technology illustrations
- carefully selected editorial/stock photography
- purpose-built graphics

### Agent-sourced images

The user does not have to supply website photography.

The coding/content agent must source suitable high-quality licensed imagery when a page needs photography or visual context.

The agent must:

- use reputable sources with appropriate licensing
- record source and license/provenance in the asset model
- choose images relevant to the actual page topic
- use sufficiently high-resolution source assets
- optimize delivered assets for the web
- provide descriptive alt text
- avoid decorative image spam
- avoid using the same image across unrelated pages

If an image cannot be sourced legitimately, use a purpose-built diagram or graphic instead of a placeholder.

### Prohibited image treatment

Never use:

- placeholder image boxes
- empty image placeholders
- fake DECHY offices
- fake DECHY employees
- fake client meetings
- fake client work
- fake project screenshots
- fake testimonials
- generic AI robots
- glowing brains
- neon neural networks
- purple/cyan futuristic clichés
- distorted interfaces
- unreadable generated text
- low-quality stock imagery
- irrelevant decorative photography

## 12. Visual Quality Gate

Before scaling a design from one representative page to the remaining site, inspect the rendered page in a browser.

Review:

- visual hierarchy
- typography size
- body readability
- section depth
- whitespace balance
- imagery
- visual rhythm
- responsive behavior
- mobile typography
- navigation
- CTAs
- cards and repeated components
- accessibility
- performance

A page that is technically valid but visually sparse is not approved.

## 13. Content Quality Gate

Before publication, ask:

1. Does the page answer its primary intent?
2. Does it explain the subject clearly?
3. Does it contain enough substance for the buyer or reader?
4. Does it demonstrate relevant expertise?
5. Does it connect to related entities naturally?
6. Does it provide useful answers for AEO?
7. Does it establish clear entity relationships for GEO?
8. Does it avoid unsupported claims?
9. Does it avoid keyword stuffing?
10. Does it avoid repetitive generic AI prose?
11. Does it have useful internal links?
12. Does it have an appropriate next step?
13. Does the visual treatment support the content?

If the answer to several questions is no, the page is not complete.
