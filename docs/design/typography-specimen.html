Build, buy, or integrate

Most organizations reach a point where off-the-shelf tools no longer fit. Spreadsheets multiply, systems stop talking to each other, and staff spend hours moving information by hand. The question is rarely whether to use software, but which combination of building, buying, and connecting produces a system people will really use and the business can maintain.

A sound answer starts with the workflow rather than the technology. We map how work moves, where data is created and consumed, and what must stay flexible. Only then do we decide what to build, what to buy, and what to connect through APIs. That order keeps the architecture simple and the maintenance burden predictable over years, not months.

What Are AI Agents and How Do They Differ from Chatbots and Automation

By a named author · Reviewed by a named reviewer · Updated with a real date · 12 min read

An AI agent is a software system that uses a language model to decide what to do next, call tools, and work toward a goal with limited step-by-step instruction. A chatbot answers; an agent acts. Automation follows fixed rules; an agent chooses among options at run time.

Why the distinction matters

The three terms are often used interchangeably, which leads to poor purchasing and design decisions. A fixed workflow is cheaper, faster, and easier to test. An agent is justified only when the path to the result cannot be fully specified in advance, and when the cost of an occasional wrong step is acceptable or guarded by review.

Failure modes to plan for

Common problems include tool misuse, loops, hidden cost growth, and unreviewed actions on live systems. Each needs a specific control, such as step limits, scoped permissions, logging, and human approval for irreversible actions. See AI security and governance for the full checklist.
