Here is what drives the cost and how to spend it wisely.

## What a marketplace actually is

A marketplace connects two groups, usually sellers (or providers) and buyers (or customers), and earns money from the transactions. Examples by type:

- **Goods:** handmade items, used products, wholesale supplies
- **Services:** freelancers, home services, tutors, consultants
- **Rentals and bookings:** accommodation, equipment, spaces, vehicles
- **B2B:** parts, materials, procurement
- **Digital products:** templates, courses, software

Each type changes the features, the risks and the budget.

## Core features

### For buyers
- Search, filters and sorting
- Listing pages with photos, descriptions, price and reviews
- Account and profile
- Messaging with sellers
- Checkout, booking or quote request
- Order history and receipts
- Reviews and ratings
- Saved items and notifications

### For sellers
- Onboarding and verification
- Listing creation and management
- Calendar, inventory or availability tools
- Order and booking management
- Messaging
- Payouts and earnings reports
- Reviews and reputation

### For the platform (admin)
- User and listing moderation
- Dispute handling and refunds
- Commission and fee settings
- Reports and analytics
- Content management
- Fraud monitoring
- Customer support tools

## What drives the cost

**Payments are the big one.** Marketplaces need to take money from buyers, hold it, take a commission and pay out sellers. This usually requires a payments platform designed for marketplaces, which handles splitting funds, identity checks for sellers and some compliance. It adds design complexity, fees and legal considerations. Get legal advice for your region, especially for holding funds.

**Search and discovery.** Simple filtering is cheap. Smart search, location-based results and recommendations cost more.

**Number of user types and platforms.** A website only is cheaper. Adding iOS and Android apps raises the cost. Many marketplaces launch with a good mobile web experience first.

**Trust and safety.** Identity checks, reviews, dispute processes and fraud tools take real effort and are essential for success.

**Messaging.** Basic in-platform messaging is moderate. Real-time chat with attachments and notifications costs more.

**Booking and availability logic.** Calendars, time zones, cancellations, deposits and double-booking prevention add complexity.

**Integrations.** Maps, shipping, tax, accounting, identity checks and email or SMS.

**Design.** A polished, trustworthy interface matters more in marketplaces than in many apps, because strangers are transacting.

## The chicken-and-egg problem

Sellers want buyers. Buyers want choice. With neither, you have an empty shop. This is why many marketplaces fail, regardless of technology.

Ways founders solve it:

- **Start narrow.** One city, one niche, one category. Be the best place for that group.
- **Seed supply first.** Recruit and onboard the first sellers personally, sometimes by creating listings for them.
- **Do it manually at first.** Run the matching by hand with a simple site, email and spreadsheets, to prove people will pay.
- **Offer sellers a reason to join.** Free tools, lower fees at the start, or access to customers they can't reach elsewhere.
- **Bring your own demand.** An existing audience, community or customer list.
- **Make one side valuable alone.** Give sellers useful software, such as booking or invoicing, so they come even before buyers do.

A technical build without a plan for the first 100 sellers and first 1,000 buyers is an expensive gamble.

## Revenue models

- **Commission** on each transaction, commonly a percentage
- **Listing fees**
- **Subscription** for sellers
- **Featured listings and ads**
- **Lead fees** for introductions
- **Buyer fees**
- **Payment and financing services**

Check that your take rate leaves room for payment fees and still leaves sellers satisfied. Disintermediation, where buyers and sellers deal off-platform after meeting, is a common threat. Give them reasons to stay: payment protection, insurance, reviews, tools and guarantees.

## Trust and safety

Strangers need reasons to trust each other.

- Seller verification
- Clear profiles and photos
- Reviews and ratings, with fraud controls
- Secure, on-platform payments
- Dispute and refund policies
- Moderation of listings and messages
- Reporting tools
- Clear terms and community rules
- Responsive support

Budget time and money for moderation and support. They do not scale themselves.

## Build vs launch on an existing platform

Before building custom, consider:

- **No-code or low-code tools** can test an idea quickly, with limits on branding, logic and scale
- **Marketplace extensions** for ecommerce platforms can work for simple product marketplaces. See our guide to [Shopify vs custom ecommerce](/resources/comparisons/shopify-vs-custom-ecommerce/).
- **Custom build** when your workflow, matching or booking logic is unique or when you need full control

A common path: test with a manual or no-code version, then build custom once demand is proven.

## A sensible plan

1. **Validate demand** with interviews and a landing page for both sides.
2. **Pick one niche and region.**
3. **Run a manual pilot** with 10 to 20 sellers and a few dozen buyers.
4. **Define the MVP:** the shortest path from search to paid transaction.
5. **Choose the payments approach** with legal advice.
6. **Build and launch** to a small group.
7. **Measure:** listings per seller, search to inquiry rate, transaction completion, repeat rate and take rate.
8. **Fix the weakest link** before adding features.
9. **Expand** to new categories or regions.

## Common mistakes

- Building a huge platform before proving anyone will transact
- Ignoring how to attract the first sellers and buyers
- Overcomplicating payments without legal advice
- Skipping trust and safety features
- Launching in too many categories at once
- Copying a famous marketplace feature for feature
- Underestimating support and moderation work
- Setting fees that make off-platform deals tempting

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to build a marketplace app?
An MVP typically costs {{fact:price.ecommerce-headless-store-or-marketplace}}. Full platforms with mobile apps and advanced features start from {{fact:price.mobile-app-development-complex-or-enterprise-app-from}}.

### How long does it take to build a marketplace?
About 3 to 5 months for an MVP, and 8 to 14 months for a full platform.

### How do marketplaces make money?
Mostly through commissions on transactions, plus fees, subscriptions, featured listings and ads.

### What is the hardest part of building a marketplace?
Attracting both sides at the same time. Technology is usually easier than building supply, demand and trust.

### Do I need a mobile app at launch?
Not always. A responsive website can validate the idea at lower cost. Add native apps when usage patterns justify them.

### How do I handle payments between buyers and sellers?
Use a payment platform designed for marketplaces that can split payments and pay out sellers. Get legal and tax advice for your region, especially if you hold funds.
