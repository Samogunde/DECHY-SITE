Here is what to look for, what to ignore and when a custom build makes sense.

## What a CRM is for in real estate

A CRM, or customer relationship manager, is the system where you keep every contact, conversation and deal. For agents and teams it should answer three questions at any moment:

1. **Who do I need to contact today?**
2. **What did we last talk about?**
3. **Which deals are close to closing, and what is blocking them?**

If a CRM doesn't make these easy, it becomes a database nobody opens.

## Features that actually matter

### 1. Fast lead capture
Leads come from many places: your website, portals, social ads, open houses, referrals and phone calls. A CRM should collect them automatically in one place, with source tracking so you know what's working.

**Why it matters:** the first agent to respond often wins the client. Speed is the biggest factor in converting online leads.

### 2. Instant alerts and routing
New leads should trigger immediate notifications by phone, text or email, and route to the right agent by area, price range, language or availability.

### 3. Automated follow-up
Most leads don't buy or sell the first time you talk. Automated email and text sequences keep you in touch for weeks and months, without manual effort. Look for:

- Welcome messages for new leads
- Drip campaigns for different stages: buyer, seller, past client
- Reminders to call
- Birthday, home anniversary and market update messages

Automation should sound human and be easy to pause when a person replies.

### 4. A clear pipeline
A visual board of deals by stage, such as new lead, contacted, showing, offer, under contract and closed. Drag and drop. At a glance, you see where each deal stands and what is overdue.

### 5. Complete contact history
Every call, email, text, note, property viewed and document in one timeline. When a client calls back after three months, you shouldn't be searching your inbox.

### 6. Tasks and reminders
Each contact and deal should have a next action and a due date. A good CRM turns "I should follow up with Maria" into a task that shows up on the right day.

### 7. Mobile app
Agents work from cars, showings and open houses. They need to:

- Add notes with voice or text
- See the day's tasks
- Call or text a client directly from the CRM, logged automatically
- Access contact details and documents

If it's clumsy on a phone, agents won't use it.

### 8. Listings and property matching
- Save buyer criteria and send matching new listings automatically
- Track which properties a client has viewed or liked
- Connect to listing feeds or your MLS, where allowed

### 9. Email, text and call integration
Communication should be logged automatically, not typed in by hand. Two-way text messaging and call recording, where legal, save time and keep a complete record.

### 10. Document and transaction tracking
Checklists for each deal, deadlines for inspections and financing, and signed documents stored with the deal. Missed deadlines can cost clients and commissions.

### 11. Reporting
Simple answers to simple questions:

- Which lead sources produce closed deals?
- How fast do we respond?
- How many leads become clients?
- What is our pipeline value?
- Which agents need support?

### 12. Team features, if you work with others
Lead distribution rules, shared calendars, permissions and agent performance views.

## Features that sound good but matter less

- A huge list of integrations you will never use
- Complicated dashboards with dozens of charts
- Heavy social media tools you don't need
- Anything that takes more than a minute to log a call

The best test: can an agent update a contact in under 30 seconds from a phone?

## Build or buy?

**Buy a ready-made real estate CRM when:**
- You are an individual agent or a small team
- Standard workflows fit how you work
- You want to start in days

**Build custom when:**
- You have an unusual process, such as property management combined with sales
- You want your own branded client portal and app
- You need to combine data from many sources in a specific way
- Subscription costs for many agents are rising
- You plan to offer the CRM to others as a product

A common middle path is to use an existing CRM and add custom automations, dashboards or a client portal on top. See our guide to [build vs buy software](/resources/guides/build-buy-or-integrate-business-software/).

## How to choose

1. **Map your process.** How does a lead become a client today?
2. **List your must-haves:** lead capture, follow-up, pipeline, mobile.
3. **Try the mobile app** before committing.
4. **Check your lead sources.** Does it connect to the portals and ad platforms you use?
5. **Ask about data export.** You should be able to take your contacts with you.
6. **Start with a trial** and have the agents who'll use it daily test it.
7. **Keep it simple.** The best CRM is the one your team actually uses.

## Making a CRM work

A CRM only helps if it is used. To get adoption:

- Pick one place where all leads go
- Train the team and set clear expectations
- Make updating the CRM part of the daily routine
- Review the pipeline in weekly meetings
- Clean the data regularly
- Measure response time and follow-up rates

## Common mistakes

- Buying a complex CRM and using 10 percent of it
- Letting leads sit in several different inboxes
- Over-automating, so messages feel cold
- Not following up after the first contact
- Ignoring data quality, which leads to duplicate and outdated contacts
- Not respecting consent and messaging laws for texts and emails

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most important feature in a real estate CRM?
Fast lead capture with instant alerts, followed by consistent follow-up. Speed and persistence win more deals than any other feature.

### How much does a real estate CRM cost?
Ready-made options cost roughly $30 to $500 a month. A custom CRM typically costs $20,000 and up.

### Do I need a custom CRM?
Most agents and small teams do well with a ready-made option. Custom makes sense for unusual workflows, branded client portals or a software product.

### Can a CRM automate follow-ups?
Yes. Drip campaigns, reminders and personalized messages can run automatically, while still letting you step in when someone replies.

### Should a CRM connect to my website?
Yes. Website forms, property inquiries and chat should feed directly into the CRM, with the source recorded.

### How long does it take to set up a CRM?
A ready-made one can be running in days. Configuring automation and integrations takes one to four weeks. A custom CRM takes several months.
