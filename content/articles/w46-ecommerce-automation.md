Here are twelve workflows worth considering, how they work and what to watch.

## Before you automate anything

Two rules save a lot of pain.

1. **Fix the process before you automate it.** A messy process automated is just a faster mess.
2. **Start with low-risk tasks.** Automate notifications and data syncing before you automate refunds or pricing.

Also decide where a person must stay in the loop, such as high-value orders, unusual returns and anything involving money going out.

## 12 workflows

### 1. Order notifications and routing
When an order arrives, the right people and systems know automatically.
- Send the order to your warehouse, 3PL or supplier
- Notify the team in chat for high-value or flagged orders
- Tag orders by type: preorder, gift, wholesale, international
- Create tasks for custom items

### 2. Inventory sync
Stock levels update across your store, marketplaces and warehouse.
- Reduce stock when an order is placed
- Pause listings when stock hits zero
- Alert you at reorder points
- Sync with supplier stock feeds for dropshipping

Stock errors cause overselling and angry customers, so this often has the highest value.

### 3. Shipping labels and tracking
- Generate labels and choose the carrier based on weight, destination or price
- Send tracking numbers to customers automatically
- Update order status when the carrier confirms delivery
- Flag delayed shipments for follow-up

### 4. Post-purchase emails
- Order confirmation and shipping updates
- Delivery check-in and how-to-use tips
- Review requests timed after delivery
- Cross-sell suggestions based on what was bought
- Replenishment reminders for consumables

### 5. Abandoned cart and browse recovery
- Email or message customers who left items in their cart
- Use a short sequence of one to three messages, not a barrage
- Include the cart contents and an easy return link
- Respect consent and unsubscribe rules

### 6. Customer segmentation
- Tag new customers, repeat buyers and high spenders automatically
- Create a VIP segment for early access
- Identify lapsed customers for win-back campaigns
- Feed segments into email, ads and support tools

### 7. Returns and refunds
- Let customers request returns through a form or portal
- Check the order against your policy
- Generate return labels
- Notify the warehouse and finance
- Issue refunds or store credit once the item arrives, with approval for exceptions

Keep a person in the loop for large refunds.

### 8. Customer support triage
- Classify incoming emails by topic: shipping, returns, product questions
- Draft replies using order data and policy text, for a person to approve
- Answer common questions automatically on chat, with a handoff to a person
- Route complaints and VIP customers to senior staff

See our guides on [AI chatbots for websites](/resources/guides/ai-chatbot-for-website/) and [AI agents vs chatbots](/resources/definitions/what-are-ai-agents/).

### 9. Product data and listings
- Import supplier product feeds
- Standardize titles, sizes and categories
- Generate first-draft descriptions and meta tags with AI for human editing
- Publish to your store and marketplaces
- Update prices and stock on a schedule

AI-written descriptions should be reviewed. Generic text hurts both conversion and search.

### 10. Fraud checks
- Flag orders with mismatched billing and shipping, unusual quantities or high-risk patterns
- Hold flagged orders for manual review
- Auto-approve low-risk orders

### 11. Accounting and finance
- Send sales data to your accounting software
- Reconcile payouts from payment providers
- Record fees and refunds
- Produce tax summaries
- Create supplier purchase orders at reorder points

### 12. Reporting and alerts
- A daily or weekly summary: revenue, orders, conversion rate, average order value, top products
- Alerts for sudden drops in sales, spikes in refunds or failed payments
- Margin by product and channel
- Ad spend against revenue

Automated reporting often saves several hours a week and surfaces problems earlier.

## Tools

You rarely need custom code to start.

- **Built-in store automations,** such as Shopify Flow or similar features in other platforms, for simple rules
- **Workflow platforms** such as n8n, Make and Zapier to connect your store, email, spreadsheet, helpdesk and accounting tools. See our comparison of [n8n, Make and Zapier](/resources/comparisons/n8n-vs-make-vs-zapier/).
- **Email and SMS platforms** for lifecycle campaigns
- **Shipping and fulfilment apps** for labels and tracking
- **Inventory management tools** for multi-channel stock
- **AI services** for drafting, classification and summaries
- **Custom code** when you need specific logic or scale

Pricing and limits change, so check current plans before you commit. Watch task counts, since high-volume stores can hit usage limits quickly.

## Where automation goes wrong

- **No error handling.** A failed step silently stops orders from reaching the warehouse. Add alerts.
- **Duplicate actions.** A workflow that fires twice sends two refunds or two emails. Build in checks.
- **Over-automated customer contact.** Customers notice robotic or badly timed messages.
- **Bad data.** Wrong SKUs, missing addresses and inconsistent tags break flows.
- **Too many tools.** Each connection is a possible failure point.
- **No owner.** Somebody must watch alerts and update workflows when tools change.
- **Compliance gaps.** Marketing messages need consent, and customer data needs protection.
- **Pricing automation without guardrails.** Automatic price changes without limits can destroy margin.

## How to start

1. **List your repetitive tasks** for a week and note how long each takes.
2. **Pick the top three** by time saved and low risk.
3. **Write each workflow as a simple list of steps** and exceptions.
4. **Build and test with sample orders** before going live.
5. **Add alerts** for failures.
6. **Run alongside the manual process** for a week.
7. **Review monthly** and add the next workflow.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I automate first in my online store?
Order notifications, inventory sync and shipping updates. They are repetitive, rule-based and have a big effect on customer experience.

### Do I need a developer to automate my store?
Not for simple workflows. Built-in tools and workflow platforms cover many needs. A developer helps for custom logic, complex integrations and high volume.

### How much time can ecommerce automation save?
Many small stores save 5 to 20 hours a week, depending on order volume and how manual their current process is.

### Is it safe to automate customer emails?
Yes for transactional and lifecycle messages with consent and unsubscribe options. Keep a person in the loop for complaints and sensitive cases.

### Can AI handle customer support for my store?
For common questions such as order status and policies, yes, with a handoff to a person. Test carefully and give it only the data and actions it needs.

### What if an automation fails?
Add monitoring and alerts so a person is notified quickly, and design workflows so a failed step can be retried safely without duplicates.
