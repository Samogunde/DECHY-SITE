Here is how to decide, what features matter and where the money goes.

## Types of e-learning platforms

**Course marketplace.** Many instructors sell courses to the public, as on large course sites.

**Single-brand course platform.** One company or educator sells its own courses, often with memberships.

**Corporate LMS.** A company trains staff, tracks compliance and certifications.

**Academy or school platform.** Classes, cohorts, assignments, grading and parent or admin access.

**Live learning.** Scheduled classes with video, whiteboards and attendance.

**Coaching and community.** Programs combining content, group discussion and one-to-one sessions.

**Skills and assessment tools.** Quizzes, simulations and certification exams.

Your type decides the features, the traffic pattern and the budget.

## Core features

### For learners
- Sign-up, login and profile
- Course catalog, search and filters
- Lesson pages with video, text, downloads and quizzes
- Progress tracking and resume where they left off
- Certificates on completion
- Notes, bookmarks and discussion
- Mobile-friendly or app access, with offline downloads where useful
- Notifications and reminders
- Payments, subscriptions or enrolment codes

### For instructors and admins
- Course builder with drag-and-drop lessons and sections
- Upload and manage video and files
- Quizzes, assignments and grading
- Drip release of content over time or by cohort
- Learner management, groups and roles
- Messaging and announcements
- Analytics: completion, drop-off points, quiz scores
- Coupons, bundles and pricing tools
- Reports and exports

### For organizations
- Single sign-on
- Teams, managers and reporting by department
- Compliance tracking and renewal reminders
- Integration with HR and other systems
- Branding and custom domains
- Standards such as SCORM or xAPI if you need to import existing content

## Features that raise completion rates

Completion is the real measure of success. Many online courses see low completion, so design for it.

- **Short lessons** of a few minutes each
- **Clear progress indicators** and next-step prompts
- **Quizzes and practice** inside lessons, not only at the end
- **Reminders** by email or notification
- **Community or cohort** features that create accountability
- **Quick feedback** from instructors or automated checks
- **Certificates and badges** that learners want to show
- **A first lesson that delivers a quick win**
- **Easy mobile access**

A beautiful platform with weak course design will not fix poor completion.

## Build, buy or combine?

### Use an existing platform when
- You mainly need to host and sell courses
- You want to launch in days or weeks
- Your budget is small
- Standard features cover your needs
- Content quality, not software, is your advantage

Hosted course platforms and LMS products often charge a monthly fee, sometimes plus a transaction fee. Many include video hosting, payments and certificates.

### Build custom when
- Your learning experience is unique, for example adaptive paths, simulations or integrated coaching
- You need deep integration with your product, CRM (customer relationship management system) or HR system
- You have complex roles, pricing or multi-tenant needs
- Per-learner platform fees are becoming expensive
- You want full control over data, branding and roadmap
- You're building a marketplace with your own commission model. See [marketplace app development cost](/resources/guides/marketplace-app-development-cost/).

### Combine
Many businesses use an existing platform for hosting and payments and build custom pieces around it, such as a branded dashboard or assessment tool. Our guide to [build vs buy software](/resources/guides/build-buy-or-integrate-business-software/) can help you decide.

## Video: the cost that surprises people

Video is the heaviest part of most platforms.

- **Hosting and streaming.** Use a video service designed for streaming, with adaptive quality, rather than serving files from your own server.
- **Cost scales with minutes watched and stored.** Model it at your expected volume.
- **Protection.** Decide how much you care about preventing downloads and sharing. Strong protection costs more and can annoy honest learners.
- **Captions and transcripts** help accessibility and search, and are required in some settings.
- **Live classes** need video conferencing integration or a streaming service.

## Accessibility and compliance

- Follow accessibility standards such as WCAG: captions, keyboard navigation, readable contrast
- Protect learner data and follow privacy laws
- If you serve children, check the specific rules on consent and data
- Schools and employers may need records, reports and data retention policies

## Content is the product

The software is a delivery vehicle. Budget for:

- Course design with clear outcomes
- Filming, editing and audio quality
- Worksheets and assessments
- Reviews with subject experts
- Updates when information changes

Weak content kills a platform faster than a weak interface.

## How to start

1. **Define the learner and the outcome.** What can they do after finishing?
2. **Pilot with one course** on an existing tool, if possible, to test demand and learn what learners need.
3. **Collect feedback and completion data.**
4. **List the limits** you hit with the tool.
5. **Define a custom MVP** that fixes those limits: the smallest feature set that gets learners through a course.
6. **Build and launch to a small cohort.**
7. **Measure completion, satisfaction and revenue,** then iterate.

See [building an MVP (minimum viable product, a first version with only the essentials)](/resources/cost-guides/how-much-does-it-cost-to-build-an-mvp/) for staging the budget.

## Common mistakes

- Building custom before testing demand
- Copying a huge course marketplace feature for feature
- Underestimating video costs
- Ignoring mobile
- Overlooking accessibility
- Forgetting instructor and admin tools
- No plan for marketing and acquiring learners
- Treating content as an afterthought
- Not tracking completion and drop-off

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to build an e-learning platform?
A custom course platform typically costs {{fact:price.web-development-advanced-web-app}}. A fuller LMS costs {{fact:price.software-development-operations-platform}}, and large platforms cost more.

### Should I build a custom LMS or use an existing one?
Use an existing one if standard features meet your needs and you want to launch quickly. Build custom when your learning model or integrations are unique, or when platform fees at scale outweigh a build.

### How long does it take to build an LMS?
About 3 to 5 months for a focused course platform, and 5 to 10 months for a fuller LMS.

### What is SCORM?
A standard for packaging online learning content so it can run in different learning management systems. You need it if you import existing corporate training modules.

### How do I host videos for an online course?
Use a dedicated video streaming service rather than your own web server. It handles quality, speed and scale. Costs depend on storage and viewing time.

### How can I increase course completion?
Use short lessons, quizzes, progress tracking, reminders, community features and quick feedback, and design courses around clear, achievable outcomes.
