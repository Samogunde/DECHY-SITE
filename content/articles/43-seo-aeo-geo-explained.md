## Three ways people now find a business

For years, being found online meant appearing in a list of search results. That still matters, but it is no longer the only way. Search engines now show direct answers at the top of the page. AI assistants answer questions in full sentences and mention the businesses they trust.

Three disciplines cover these routes. Search engine optimization (SEO) helps a site appear and rank in search results. Answer engine optimization (AEO) helps a site supply the direct answers search engines show. Generative engine optimization (GEO) helps AI assistants understand a business correctly and describe it accurately.

They overlap more than the names suggest. All three reward the same foundation: a site that is easy to read, clear about what it offers, and consistent in what it says.

## What SEO covers

Search engine optimization is the oldest of the three. It has two broad parts. The technical part makes sure search engines can find, load, and understand every page. That means fast pages, clean links, sensible addresses, and no accidental barriers that hide content.

The content part makes sure each page answers what people actually search for. A page about a service should explain what it is, who it is for, and why it matters, in the words customers use. Links from other respected sites still matter too, because they signal that others trust the content.

- Pages load quickly and work on every device
- Every page can be found and read by search engines
- Each page answers a clear question in plain language
- Other respected sites link to yours

## What AEO covers

Answer engine optimization focuses on the direct answers that appear above ordinary results. These include short definitions, steps, comparisons, and question-and-answer panels. Search engines pull them from pages that state an answer clearly and early.

Good answer pages do a few things well. They ask the question in plain words, answer it in the first sentence or two, and then add detail. They use headings that match real questions. They mark up questions and answers with structured data, which is a standard way of labeling content so machines can read it reliably.

## What GEO covers

Generative engine optimization is the newest discipline. When someone asks an AI assistant for a recommendation or an explanation, the assistant draws on what it has learned and, increasingly, on live search. GEO is about making sure that what it finds about your business is accurate, consistent, and easy to quote.

AI assistants favor sources that are clear, specific, and consistent. If your services, location, and facts are described the same way across your site and other trusted places, the assistant is more likely to describe you correctly. If they conflict, it may guess, and guesses can be wrong.

## How the three work together

It helps to think of one foundation with three uses. Clear, accurate, well-structured pages rank in search, supply direct answers, and give AI assistants something reliable to quote. Work done for one usually helps the others.

There are differences in emphasis. SEO pays close attention to keywords and links. AEO cares most about question-shaped headings and concise answers. GEO cares most about consistent facts and clearly described entities. An entity is a thing a search engine or AI can identify, such as your company, a service, or a place. A plan that covers all three avoids optimizing for one at the expense of the others.

- One foundation: fast, readable, accurate pages
- SEO adds search intent and trusted links
- AEO adds clear questions and early answers
- GEO adds consistent facts and well-described entities

## An example of the three together

Consider a company that builds booking software for clinics. A practice manager searches "online booking system for clinics". Strong SEO helps the company's service page appear in the results, because the page loads quickly, is easy to read, and uses the words practice managers use.

The same manager then asks "how much does clinic booking software cost?" Good AEO means a clear answer near the top of a relevant page, perhaps a range and the factors that move it, which the search engine may show directly. Later, the manager asks an AI assistant to suggest suppliers. Good GEO means the company is described consistently across its own site and trusted directories, so the assistant can name it correctly and explain what it does.

One set of well-made pages supported all three moments. Nothing was written only for a machine. Each step simply made the company easier to find, understand, and trust.

## Common mistakes

The most common mistake is writing for machines instead of people. Pages stuffed with keywords read badly and are increasingly ignored. Another is creating many thin pages that say almost the same thing, which dilutes trust rather than building it.

A third is inconsistency. A company described one way on its website, another way on directories, and a third way in old articles leaves search engines and AI assistants unsure what is true. A fourth is neglecting the technical basics. A beautifully written page that loads slowly or cannot be read by search engines will struggle whatever its content.

## What we check first

When we review a site, we start with the basics that affect all three disciplines. We check whether search engines can reach and read every important page, how fast pages load on a phone, and whether each page states clearly what it offers. We look for duplicate or near-empty pages that dilute trust.

Then we compare the facts. Is the company name, description, and list of services the same on the website, in directories, and in structured data? Where they differ, we decide which version is correct and make everything match. These checks are not glamorous, but they often deliver the quickest improvements.

## Measuring progress honestly

Visibility should be measured against business results, not vanity figures. Useful measures include how often your pages appear for the searches that matter, how many visitors arrive from search, how many of them get in touch, and whether AI assistants describe you accurately when asked.

Results take time. Technical fixes can show effect within weeks. Content and reputation build over months. Be wary of anyone promising first place quickly. Search engines and AI assistants decide rankings, and no honest provider controls them.

## Where to start

Begin with a check of the foundation. Can every important page be found and read? Do pages load quickly? Does each service page clearly say what you do, for whom, and where? Are your basic facts consistent everywhere they appear?

Then turn to the questions customers ask before they buy. Answer each one clearly on the most relevant page, in plain language, with the answer near the top. Mark up questions and key facts with structured data. Finally, keep the content current, because outdated pages lose trust with people and machines alike. ## Common questions

### Is SEO still worth doing now that AI answers questions?

Yes. AI assistants and direct answers still rely heavily on pages that search engines can find and trust. Strong SEO is the base for both.

### Do we need separate content for AI assistants?

Usually not. Clear, accurate, consistent pages serve people, search engines, and AI assistants together.

### How long until we see results?

Technical fixes can help within weeks. Content and reputation improvements usually build over several months.

### Can anyone guarantee a top ranking?

No. Search engines and AI providers decide what they show. Honest work improves your chances and your measurement, not a guarantee.
