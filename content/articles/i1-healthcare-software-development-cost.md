## Why healthcare software costs more

The same booking tool costs more in a clinic than in a hair salon, and the reason is the data. Health information is among the most sensitive data a business can hold. In the United States, HIPAA, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, sets rules for how it is stored, shared, and logged. Other regions have their own rules, such as the GDPR (the EU data protection law) for European patients.

Those rules touch every part of the software. Access must be limited to the people who need it. Every view and change of a record must be logged. Backups must be encrypted. Suppliers who handle the data must sign agreements accepting responsibility for it. None of this is optional, so it is always in the quote.

## The five cost drivers that are specific to healthcare

The first is privacy and security. Encryption, role-based access, audit logs, session timeouts, and secure hosting add roughly 10 to 15 percent to a project compared with the same features elsewhere.

The second is records integration. Most clinics run an electronic health record (EHR) system, the software that holds patient histories. Connecting to it usually means working with a healthcare data standard called FHIR, which defines how clinical systems exchange information, and passing the vendor's own approval process. Each connection costs $10,000 to $32,000.

The third is identity. Patients must be verified before they see their records, usually with a one-time code or an identity check.

The fourth is accessibility. Patients include older people and people with visual or motor impairments. Larger text, high contrast, screen reader support, and simple flows are built in from the start.

The fifth is clinical review. Clinicians must check that workflows, wording, and alerts are safe. We plan time for that review rather than discovering it late.

- Privacy and security controls on every screen
- Connections to clinical record systems
- Patient identity verification
- Accessibility for every patient
- Clinical review of workflows and wording

## What is included in the price

Every healthcare quote from us includes a data map showing where patient information lives and who can see it, plus a written list of the privacy controls we will build. It includes the design of patient and staff screens, tested with real patients and clinicians where possible. The build covers audit logging and role-based access from the first release. Before launch we run a security review and accessibility checks, and we document the controls for your compliance records.

## A typical timeline

Booking and reminders go live in four to six weeks. A patient portal takes six to ten weeks, with records integration often running alongside. Telehealth apps take eight to twelve weeks because video, scheduling, and payments must all work on every device. Vendor approval for records integration can add a few weeks that are outside anyone's control, so we plan the rest of the work around it.

## A worked example

Picture a group of four outpatient clinics. Patients book by phone, forms are filled in on paper in the waiting room, and staff retype them into the records system. No-shows cost several appointments a day.

The first project combines online booking, reminders by text and email, and digital intake forms that patients complete before the visit. Completed forms flow into the records system through its standard interface, and every access is logged. With HIPAA readiness included, it falls in the $32,000 to $44,000 range and launches in about eight weeks. Reception stops retyping forms, and reminders cut no-shows.

A second phase adds a patient portal with test results and secure messaging, quoted once the first phase is in use.

## How to keep healthcare software affordable

Start with administrative workflows such as booking, reminders, intake, and payments before clinical ones, because they touch less sensitive data and pay back within months. They deliver quick savings and carry less regulatory risk. Use established, compliant services for video, messaging, and identity checks rather than building your own. Connect to your records system through its standard interfaces rather than custom database access.

## How to compare healthcare software quotes

Healthcare quotes differ most in what they leave out. Ask each supplier to show where patient data will be stored, who can see it, and how every access is logged. Ask whether the hosting provider will sign a business associate agreement, the contract HIPAA requires when a supplier handles patient data. Ask how the software will be tested for accessibility, and whether real patients will try it before launch.

For records integration, ask whether the supplier has already worked with your EHR vendor's interfaces, and how long the vendor's approval usually takes. For telehealth, ask which video service they will use and whether it is approved for health data.

A quote that cannot answer these questions in writing is usually cheaper for a reason.

- Where patient data lives and who can see it
- A signed business associate agreement for hosting
- Accessibility testing with real patients
- Experience with your records system's interfaces
- A video service approved for health data

## Common questions

### How much does a patient portal cost?

A patient portal with records, messages, forms, and payments usually costs $24,000 to $48,000 and takes six to ten weeks.

### What does HIPAA compliance add to the cost?

Expect $6,000 to $16,000 for the security review, audit logging, and access controls, and about 10 to 15 percent more across the build for privacy-aware design and testing.

### Can you connect to our EHR system?

Usually, yes, through the system's standard interfaces. The cost depends on the vendor and ranges from $10,000 to $32,000 per system.

### How much does a telehealth app cost?

A telehealth app with video visits, scheduling, and payments on iPhone, Android, and web costs $36,000 to $72,000.

### Do you sign business associate agreements?

Yes. Where we handle protected health information, we sign the agreement the law requires and host data with providers that do the same.

### What does healthcare software cost to run after launch?

Plan for secure hosting, usually $100 to $500 a month for a clinic group, plus a care plan from $1,000 a month that covers security updates, monitoring, and audit log reviews.
