## Why the pricing model matters

The way a project is priced decides who carries the risk, how easily the plan can change, and how much you know about the final bill on day one. The same work can feel cheap or expensive depending on how well the model fits it.

There is no best model in general. There is a best model for a particular piece of work, and many projects use two: a fixed price for a defined first release, then a dedicated team or time and materials for the work that follows.

## Fixed price

A fixed price means the supplier agrees to deliver a written scope for one price. You know the cost before work starts, and if the work takes longer than planned, the supplier absorbs it.

That certainty depends on the scope. A fixed price only works when the features, screens, integrations, and acceptance rules can be written down in advance. Suppliers add a margin for risk, so a fixed price is rarely the cheapest way to buy hours, but it is often the cheapest way to buy a defined result.

Changes are handled in writing. If you add a feature, the supplier quotes it separately, and you decide whether it is worth it.

- Best when the scope is clear
- Budget certainty from the start
- Changes quoted separately
- Payment in milestones tied to working software

## Time and materials

Time and materials means you pay for the hours actually worked, at agreed hourly rates. You can change direction at any time, because nothing is locked in. You also carry the risk: if the work takes longer, the bill grows.

This model suits research, discovery, and products where the answer is genuinely unknown. It works best with a weekly report of hours and progress, and a monthly cap that cannot be exceeded without your approval.

## Dedicated team

A dedicated team is a group of people who work only on your product, every month, billed as a monthly fee. You set priorities every one or two weeks, and the team builds, tests, and releases continuously.

It suits products that change constantly: a software as a service (SaaS) product with paying customers, a platform that adds features every month, or a company that needs engineering capacity without hiring. The team learns your product deeply, which makes each month more productive than the last.

## How we use the three models

We quote fixed prices for defined projects: websites, web apps, mobile apps, stores, integrations, and a first version of a product, often called a minimum viable product (MVP). Most of our projects start this way. For discovery work, where the goal is to find the right answer, we use time and materials with a weekly cap. When a product is live and growing, we offer a dedicated team, so the people who built it keep improving it.

## What the numbers look like

Hourly rates for time and materials range from $30 an hour for quality assurance to $100 an hour for a solutions architect, with most engineering at $40 to $70. A dedicated team costs from $6,000 a month for one engineer to $55,000 or more for a scale team of four to six engineers with design, delivery, QA, and DevOps (the work of keeping software deployed, monitored, and running) support.

Fixed prices follow the ranges on our service pages: $3,000 to $7,000 for a business website, $10,000 to $20,000 for a mobile app MVP, and $15,000 to $100,000 or more for custom software.

## What each model asks of you

A fixed price asks for decisions up front. You need to agree the scope, review designs promptly, and accept that new ideas become new quotes. In return you get a known cost and a delivery date.

Time and materials asks for attention. You review progress every week, decide what comes next, and keep an eye on the hours against the cap. In return you get complete flexibility.

A dedicated team asks for a product owner on your side, someone who sets priorities every sprint and answers the team's questions quickly. In return you get people who know your product inside out and ship improvements every week.

## A worked example

Picture a company launching a new client portal. The first release is clearly defined: logins, documents, requests, and status updates. It is quoted as a fixed price of $32,000 and delivered in seven weeks.

Once clients use it, ideas pour in. Rather than quoting each change, the company moves to a starter squad of two engineers and a part-time delivery lead at about $14,000 a month. Priorities are set every two weeks, and the portal improves continuously. When a large new module is needed, it is quoted as a fixed price again.

## How to choose

Ask three questions. Can the work be written down in enough detail to price it? If yes, use a fixed price. Will the work change as you learn? If yes, use time and materials with a cap. Will there be work every month for the foreseeable future? If yes, use a dedicated team.

## Common questions

### Is fixed price more expensive than time and materials?

It includes a margin for the supplier's risk, so the hourly equivalent is higher. But the total is known in advance, and overruns are not your problem.

### Can we switch models during a project?

Yes. A common pattern is a fixed price for the first release, then a dedicated team once the product is live.

### How do you stop time and materials from running over?

With a monthly cap, a weekly report of hours and progress, and approval before any extra hours are spent.

### What is the minimum term for a dedicated team?

We ask for one month's notice. Most teams stay for six months or more because the product keeps growing.

### Do you charge for discovery?

A short call and a written plan are free. Deeper discovery, such as user research or technical audits, is quoted as a small fixed price or billed as time and materials.
