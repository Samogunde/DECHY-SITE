## Why real estate software is priced differently

Real estate software serves people with very different goals. Buyers and renters want to search and enquire. Tenants want to pay rent and report a leaking tap. Owners want to know what their property earned this month. Agents and property managers want all of it in one place without retyping anything. Each audience needs its own screens and its own view of the data, and that is the main reason prices vary.

Listing data adds a second layer. Listings usually arrive from a multiple listing service (MLS), the shared database agents use to publish properties, or from portal feeds, and each source has its own format and display rules.

## The five cost drivers that are specific to real estate

The first is listing feeds. Displaying live listings on your own website usually happens through IDX, the agreement and data feed that lets agents show MLS listings on their sites. Feeds differ by region, update on their own schedules, and come with rules about what must be shown and how. Each feed costs $3,000 to $10,000 to connect properly.

The second is search. Buyers expect filters, map search, saved searches, and alerts when a matching property appears. Good search is one of the most valuable parts of an agency website, and it costs $2,500 to $7,000 to do well.

The third is the number of audiences. A portal that serves owners, tenants, and staff costs 20 to 30 percent more than a tool for one audience, because each needs its own permissions and screens.

The fourth is money. Rent collection, deposits, owner payouts, and management fees mean money flows in several directions. Each flow needs clear records and reconciliation.

The fifth is documents. Leases, inspection reports, and notices need storage, electronic signatures, and a clear history of who sent what and when.

- Listing feeds and their display rules
- Search, maps, and alerts
- Owners, tenants, and staff in one system
- Rent, deposits, and owner payouts
- Leases, inspections, and signatures

## What is included in the price

Every real estate quote from us includes a map of your audiences and what each one sees, a plan for listing data and how often it refreshes, and the design of search, listing, and enquiry pages. Portals include role-based access, payment setup, document storage, and notifications. Before launch we test with real listings and real tenants or owners where possible, set up analytics on enquiries, and train your team to manage properties and users.

## A typical timeline

An agency website with live listings goes live in two to four weeks, depending on how quickly the feed provider approves access. A tenant app takes five to eight weeks. A property management portal takes six to ten weeks, often launched first for tenants and then for owners.

## How to keep the cost down

Use the listing tools your feed provider already offers for basic display, and invest in search and enquiry handling where you compete. Launch the portal for one audience first, usually tenants, because repair requests and rent payments save staff time immediately. Use proven services for payments and signatures. Connect your accounting package rather than building owner statements from scratch.

- Start with one audience
- Buy payments and signatures, do not build them
- Connect accounting for owner statements
- Spend on search, where agencies compete

## A worked example

Picture a property manager looking after 600 rental units for about 150 owners. Rent arrives by bank transfer and is matched by hand. Repair requests come by phone and text. Owners call every month asking for their statements.

The first phase is a tenant app and staff portal. Tenants pay rent by card or direct debit, report repairs with photos, and receive notices. Staff see every request in one queue and assign contractors. Payments reconcile automatically. That phase falls in the $28,000 to $36,000 range and launches in about seven weeks.

The second phase adds an owner area with monthly statements generated from the accounting package and a dashboard of occupancy and income. It costs about $10,000 and removes most of the monthly calls.

## How to compare real estate software quotes

Ask whether the supplier has worked with your listing feed before and how long approval usually takes. Ask how search will perform with your full list of properties, not a demo of twenty. For portals, ask how permissions work when one owner has several properties or one property has several owners. Ask where tenant and owner data is stored and who can see it. Confirm who owns the listing data, the code, and the payment provider account.

## Off-the-shelf property software or custom?

Off-the-shelf property management products suit many small agencies, and they are often the right first step. Custom software makes sense when your process differs from the product's, when you need to connect several existing tools, or when per-unit licence fees grow faster than your portfolio. A common middle path is to keep a property management product and build a custom tenant app or owner portal on top of it.

## Common questions

### How much does a real estate website with listings cost?

An agency website with live listings, search, and maps costs $6,000 to $16,000 and takes two to four weeks.

### How much does a property management portal cost?

A portal for owners, tenants, and staff with payments, repairs, and documents costs $20,000 to $48,000.

### Can you connect our MLS or portal feed?

Usually, yes. Connecting a listing feed costs $3,000 to $10,000, plus the feed provider's own fees.

### Can tenants pay rent through the app?

Yes. We connect a payment provider for card and direct debit payments, with automatic matching against each lease.

### How long does it take to build a tenant app?

Five to eight weeks for iPhone and Android, including rent payments, repair requests, and notices.

### What does real estate software cost to run?

Listing feed providers charge their own monthly fees, usually modest for one office. Hosting for a portal costs $50 to $300 a month, and a care plan for an app in daily use starts at $1,000 a month, covering updates, monitoring, and improvements.
