## Why software needs maintenance

Software does not wear out, but everything around it changes. Apple and Google release new versions of iOS and Android every year. Browsers update every few weeks. The frameworks and libraries your software is built on release security fixes. Payment providers and other services change how their connections work. Each change can break something that worked perfectly the day before.

Maintenance keeps your software working through those changes, protects it from new security threats, and gives you a steady flow of improvements as your business learns what it needs.

## The four kinds of maintenance work

Updates keep the platform, libraries, and plugins current, so known security holes are closed.

Monitoring watches uptime, errors, and performance, and alerts someone before customers notice a problem.

Fixes deal with the bugs that real use always uncovers, and with breakages caused by changes in other systems.

Improvements are small changes and new features: a new report, a better form, a faster page. This is where most of the value lies, and where a fixed number of hours each month pays off.

- Updates and security patches
- Monitoring, backups, and alerts
- Fixes for bugs and broken connections
- Improvements, in a fixed number of hours

## The 15 to 25 percent rule

A useful rule of thumb is to budget 15 to 25 percent of the build cost each year for maintenance. A $40,000 web app needs $6,000 to $10,000 a year, and more if the business wants regular improvements.

Small websites break the rule. Their build cost is low, but some work, such as backups, uptime monitoring, and security updates, costs the same however small the site is. That is why our smallest plan starts at {{fact:price.care-essential-from}} a month.

## Running costs that are not maintenance

Hosting is the cost of the servers or cloud services your software runs on. For most business websites and apps it is modest, from $20 to $300 a month. Third-party services, such as email delivery, maps, search, payment processing, and AI usage, are billed by their providers. App store accounts cost $99 a year for Apple and a one-time $25 for Google.

We list all of these separately and, wherever possible, set them up in your name.

## What happens if you skip maintenance

Unmaintained software rarely fails all at once. It degrades. A plugin stops working after a browser update. An app crashes on the newest phones. A security patch is missed, and the site is compromised. Eventually, catching up costs more than steady maintenance would have, and sometimes a rebuild is the only option.

## What is included in our care plans

Essential care suits websites and simple web apps: updates, daily backups, uptime monitoring, security patches, and up to three hours of small changes each month. Growth care suits web and mobile apps in daily use: everything in Essential, plus iPhone and Android release updates, error tracking, a monthly report, and 10 to 20 hours of improvements. Product care suits business-critical systems: a one-hour response target for urgent issues, on-call cover, performance tuning, and 40 or more hours of development a month.

## How to keep maintenance costs down

Build it well the first time. Software with automated tests, clear documentation, and few unnecessary dependencies is cheaper to maintain for its whole life. A low build price that skips these things usually costs more within a year.

Use fewer plugins and third-party services. Every dependency is one more thing to update and one more thing that can break. We often replace several overlapping plugins with a small piece of custom code that never needs a subscription.

Batch small changes. Collecting requests and releasing them together each month is cheaper than a stream of one-off fixes.

Plan major updates before they become urgent. Framework versions are supported for a set number of years, and moving to the next one on a planned schedule costs a fraction of an emergency upgrade.

- Automated tests and documentation from the start
- Fewer plugins and services
- Monthly batches of small changes
- Planned upgrades before support ends

## A worked example

Picture a services company with a customer portal built two years ago by another supplier. Nobody has updated it since launch. It still works, but it runs on an outdated framework version with known security issues, and a payment provider has announced it will retire the connection the portal uses.

The first step is a one-off catch-up: updating the framework, replacing the payment connection, and adding monitoring, quoted at about $5,000. After that, the portal moves to Growth care at $1,500 a month, including 12 hours of improvements. The company now gets a monthly report, and the backlog of small requests finally moves.

## Common questions

### How much does it cost to maintain an app per year?

For a web or mobile app in daily use, plan $12,000 to $30,000 a year, which is our Growth care plan. Simple apps can cost less; business-critical platforms cost more.

### Can you maintain software another company built?

Yes. We start with a short technical review, fix anything urgent, and then move the software onto a care plan.

### Are unused hours carried over?

Unused improvement hours carry over for one month, so a quiet month is not wasted.

### What is the response time for urgent problems?

Product care includes a one-hour response target for urgent issues. Growth care responds within one business day, and Essential care within two.

### Do we have to sign a long contract?

No. Care plans run month to month with one month's notice.

### Is maintenance included in the build price?

No. The build covers everything up to launch, plus a short period of fixes. Maintenance is a separate monthly plan, so you choose the level of care that fits.

### Can we pause a care plan during a quiet season?

Yes. Seasonal businesses can drop to Essential care for the quiet months and move back up before the busy season, with one month's notice.
