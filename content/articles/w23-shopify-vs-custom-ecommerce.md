The wrong choice costs money either way. Here is how to think it through.

## The options

**Hosted platforms** such as Shopify, BigCommerce and Wix Commerce. You pay a monthly fee and use their store software, themes and apps.

**Open-source platforms** such as WooCommerce and Magento. You host and manage them yourself, with more control and more responsibility.

**Headless commerce.** A platform like Shopify handles products, checkout and orders in the background, while a custom front end, such as one built with Next.js, controls the shopping experience.

**Fully custom.** A store built from scratch for your needs, using your own database and logic.

## Why Shopify works for most stores

- **Fast to launch.** A professional store can be live in days or weeks.
- **Everything included.** Hosting, security, checkout, payments, inventory and tax tools.
- **Proven checkout.** Shopify's checkout is widely trusted and converts well.
- **Large app ecosystem.** Thousands of apps add features, from reviews to subscriptions.
- **Low technical burden.** No servers to manage or software to patch.
- **Reliability.** The platform handles traffic spikes.

For a store selling physical products with normal pricing, shipping and payments, it is hard to beat.

## Where Shopify can hold you back

- **Ongoing fees.** Monthly plans, app subscriptions and transaction fees add up as you grow. Check the current plan pricing before you commit.
- **Customization limits.** Checkout and some data structures are restricted. Deep customization can be difficult or costly.
- **App sprawl.** Many stores bolt on a dozen apps, each costing money and slowing the site.
- **Complex pricing and rules.** Customer-specific pricing, complex bundles, quote requests or unusual B2B workflows can be awkward.
- **Data ownership and lock-in.** Your store lives on their platform.
- **Unique business models.** Marketplaces, rentals, configurators and highly custom products often strain a standard platform.

## When custom makes sense

A custom build is worth serious consideration when:

- **Your business model is unusual.** A marketplace with many sellers, a rental or booking platform, a subscription with complex rules or a custom product configurator.
- **You need deep integrations.** Connecting your store to custom ERP (enterprise resource planning system), warehouse, pricing or CRM systems in specific ways.
- **Your pricing logic is complex.** Contract pricing, tiered rules, dynamic quotes and approvals.
- **You have very high volume,** and platform fees exceed the cost of running your own.
- **Your experience is your brand.** A highly custom shopping journey is part of what sets you apart.
- **You need full control** over data, performance and the roadmap.
- **You are hitting the limits** of workarounds and apps.

If none of these apply, a custom build is probably premature.

## Break-even thinking

Compare total cost over three to five years, not just the first year.

- Add up platform fees, apps, transaction fees and workarounds on the hosted platform
- Add the build, hosting, maintenance and developer costs of custom
- Consider the revenue impact of better speed, conversion or features

For many small stores, the hosted platform wins easily. For larger or unusual stores, custom can be cheaper over time, and gives you capabilities that drive revenue.

## Speed, SEO and conversion

- **Hosted platforms** perform well by default, though heavy themes and many apps slow them.
- **Custom and headless builds** can be extremely fast and give you full control over structure, metadata and technical SEO (search engine optimization).
- **Conversion** depends on design, trust, shipping, price and checkout, not on the platform alone. A well-run Shopify store often beats a poorly built custom one.

## A practical path

1. **Start on a hosted platform** if you're new, testing a product or want speed.
2. **Keep it lean.** Use few apps and a clean theme.
3. **Track the limits.** Note every workaround, every fee and every feature you can't get.
4. **Consider headless** if you want a custom front end while keeping a proven back end.
5. **Go fully custom** when the business case is clear, and the limits cost you more than a build would.
6. **Plan the migration carefully:** products, customers, order history, URLs and SEO.

## Decision questions

- Do I sell products in a standard way?
- Do I need to launch within weeks?
- How much developer budget do I have?
- Does my pricing or workflow need logic a platform can't handle?
- How much am I paying in fees and apps today?
- Is my store experience a competitive advantage?
- Who will maintain it?

## Common mistakes

- Building custom too early, because it sounds more serious
- Staying on a platform long after it has become a bottleneck
- Installing too many apps, which slows the site and raises the cost
- Ignoring migration planning, which can hurt search rankings
- Comparing only the build price, not the five-year cost
- Skipping security and payment compliance on custom builds

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Shopify good for a small business?
Yes. It's fast to launch, reliable and easy to manage, which suits most small stores.

### When should I move from Shopify to custom?
When your business model, pricing logic or integrations can't be handled well, or when fees and workarounds cost more than a custom build would.

### How much does a custom ecommerce site cost?
Typically from {{fact:price.ecommerce-headless-store-or-marketplace-from}}, depending on features, integrations and scale.

### What is headless ecommerce?
A setup where a platform such as Shopify runs products, checkout and orders in the background, while a custom front end controls the shopping experience.

### Is custom ecommerce better for SEO?
It can offer more control and speed, but a well-configured hosted store can rank very well. Content and structure matter more than the platform.

### Can I migrate from Shopify without losing rankings?
Yes, with a careful plan: matching URLs, redirects, preserved content and monitoring after launch.
