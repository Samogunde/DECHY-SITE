## What AI integration means in practice

AI integration means putting an AI model to work inside the tools and processes you already have. For most businesses, that is not building a model from scratch. It is connecting a proven model from a provider such as OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google to your own data, and wrapping it in rules, checks, and screens your team can trust.

The price reflects that work. The model itself is rented. You pay for connecting it to the right information, deciding what it may and may not do, testing its answers, and making it easy to use.

## The four types of AI project

An AI assistant answers questions from your own content, such as policies, product manuals, or past support tickets. It uses a technique called retrieval augmented generation (RAG), which finds the relevant passages first and then writes an answer from them, so replies are grounded in your documents rather than guesswork.

An AI feature adds intelligence to a product you already run: summaries of long records, smarter search, automatic tagging, or draft replies a person approves.

An AI agent goes further and takes actions, such as creating a ticket, updating a record, or sending an email, within limits you set. Agents need careful permissions and a person to approve anything that matters.

A custom model or AI platform is trained or tuned on your own data, usually for prediction, classification, or vision. It suits businesses with large, specific datasets and a problem general models handle poorly.

## What moves the price

Your data matters most. Clean, well-organized documents make an assistant quick to build. Scattered files, scanned PDFs, and inconsistent records take time to prepare.

The systems involved come next. An assistant that reads documents is simpler than an agent that writes to your customer relationship management (CRM) system, your ticketing tool, and your inbox.

Accuracy requirements change the testing effort. A tool that drafts marketing ideas needs light checks. A tool that answers customers about prices or policies needs a test set of real questions, measured accuracy, and a clear route to a person.

- The state of your data
- The systems the AI reads from and writes to
- How accurate it must be, and who reviews it
- Expected volume, which drives running costs

## What is included in the price

Every AI quote from us starts with a short workshop to choose the one use case worth doing first. We then prepare your data, connect the model, and build the screens or integrations your team uses. Before launch we test against a set of real questions and report accuracy, so you know how well it performs. Launch includes usage monitoring, cost alerts, and a review process for answers that need a human.

## A typical timeline

An assistant on your content goes live in one to three weeks. An AI feature in your product takes two to four weeks. Agents take four to six weeks, because each action they can take is tested separately. Custom models take two to three months, including data preparation and evaluation.

## A worked example

Picture a property management company with a support team answering the same tenant questions every day: rent dates, repair requests, parking rules, and move-out steps. Answers live in a policy folder and old emails.

The first project is an AI assistant on the company's own documents, available to staff first and tenants second. It answers with a link to the source passage, and hands anything about money or disputes to a person. It falls in the $8,000 to $12,000 range and launches in about two weeks, with usage costs of around $60 a month at their volume.

Once it proves reliable, a second project lets it create repair tickets automatically, turning it into a simple agent.

## How to keep AI costs under control

Start with one use case that saves measurable time. Measure accuracy before and after launch. Cap usage with alerts so a spike never becomes a surprise bill. Keep a person in the loop for any answer involving money, health, legal matters, or personal data.

## How to compare AI quotes

AI quotes vary more than most, because some suppliers price only the connection to the model and leave data preparation, testing, and monitoring out. Ask each supplier how they will prepare your data, how they will measure accuracy, and what happens when the AI does not know the answer.

Ask which model provider they plan to use, why, and how easily it could be swapped later. Providers change their prices and models often, and you should not be locked in. Ask for an estimate of monthly usage costs at your expected volume, written into the quote. Finally, confirm that you own the prompts, the data pipeline, and the code, so another team could run the system if needed.

## Common questions

### How much does it cost to add ChatGPT to my website?

A chat assistant that answers from your own content costs $5,000 to $15,000 to build, plus usage fees that are usually modest for a typical business.

### Do we need our own AI model?

Rarely. Most business problems are solved by connecting a proven model to your data. Custom models make sense only for large, specific datasets.

### Is our data safe with an AI provider?

We use provider plans that do not train on your data, limit what the AI can access, and log what it does. Sensitive data can be masked before it reaches the model.

### How accurate will it be?

We measure it. Before launch, we test against real questions from your business and report the result, then improve the weak spots.

### What is the cheapest way to start with AI?

A short workshop to choose the highest-value task, followed by an assistant on your own documents. It is quick to build and easy to measure.
