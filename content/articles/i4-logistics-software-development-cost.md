## Why logistics software is priced differently

Logistics software lives in the real world. Drivers work in poor signal, in a hurry, often with gloves on. Customers want to know exactly when a parcel will arrive. Planners juggle vehicles, time windows, and last-minute changes. Every one of those conditions shapes the software and its cost.

Logistics also depends on other systems. Orders arrive from stores and enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems, the software that runs a company's orders, stock, and finance. Shipments move to carriers. Stock sits in a warehouse management system (WMS). The value of logistics software lies in connecting all of them, and connections are where much of the budget goes.

## The five cost drivers that are specific to logistics

The first is live location. Showing vehicles on a map is easy for ten vans. For two hundred vans sending a position every fifteen seconds, the system must store and display thousands of updates an hour without slowing down.

The second is offline use. Driver apps must keep working when the signal drops and sync when it returns, without losing a single proof of delivery. Building that reliably adds $4,000 to $12,000.

The third is proof of delivery. Photos, signatures, barcodes, and timestamps must be captured quickly and stored safely, because they settle disputes and trigger invoices.

The fourth is partner systems. Carriers, warehouses, and customers' ERP systems each use their own formats, and many are older systems without modern interfaces. Each connection costs $8,000 to $25,000.

The fifth is exceptions. Failed deliveries, damaged goods, refused parcels, and returns each need their own flow. They are a small share of deliveries but a large share of cost and customer complaints, so they deserve proper design.

- Live location at your real fleet size
- Driver apps that work offline
- Proof of delivery that settles disputes
- Connections to carriers, warehouses, and ERPs
- Clear flows for failed deliveries and returns

## What is included in the price

Every logistics quote from us includes a delivery flow map from order to proof of delivery to invoice, with every system and person involved. The build covers the driver app, the planner dashboard, notifications, and the integrations you named. Before launch we test with real drivers on real routes, including dead spots, and run a pilot with a small part of the fleet before rolling out to everyone.

## A typical timeline

A customer tracking portal goes live in three to six weeks. A driver app takes six to ten weeks, including a pilot. A dispatch and tracking platform takes two to four months, delivered in stages: driver app and proof of delivery first, then planning and tracking, then optimization. Route optimization adds four to eight weeks depending on the rules involved.

## How to keep the cost down

Start where the paperwork hurts most, usually proof of delivery. Use proven mapping, routing, and messaging services rather than building your own. Connect to partners through their standard interfaces where they exist, and keep custom integration for the systems that matter most. Pilot with a few drivers before rolling out, because drivers find problems no meeting room ever will.

## A worked example

Picture a regional delivery company with 60 vans. Drivers carry paper run sheets, proof of delivery is a signature on paper, and customers call the office to ask where their delivery is. Invoices wait until the paperwork comes back.

The first phase is a driver app with jobs, navigation links, offline proof of delivery with photos and signatures, and a planner dashboard with live van positions. Customers get a tracking link with an arrival window. It falls in the $50,000 to $65,000 range and launches in about nine weeks, after a two-week pilot with eight drivers.

Calls to the office drop sharply, disputes are settled with a photo, and invoices go out the same day. Route optimization follows as a second phase once the data shows where time is lost.

## What logistics software pays back

The return usually comes from four places. Fewer calls to the office, because customers can see where their delivery is. Faster invoicing, because proof of delivery arrives the moment the job is done. Fewer disputes, because every delivery has a photo, a signature, and a timestamp. And more stops per driver, once routes are planned with real data rather than habit.

Measure those four numbers before the project starts, so you can see the change after launch. They also help decide what to build next: if calls drop but disputes stay high, the next phase should focus on proof of delivery and exceptions.

## How to compare logistics software quotes

Ask how the driver app behaves with no signal, and ask to see it working in airplane mode. Ask how the system performs at your real fleet size and delivery volume, not a demo. Ask which carriers and warehouse systems the supplier has connected before. Ask how failed deliveries and returns will work. Confirm that you own the code, the delivery data, and the mapping and messaging accounts.

## Common questions

### How much does a delivery driver app cost?

A driver app with jobs, navigation links, offline proof of delivery, and photos costs $25,000 to $60,000 and takes six to ten weeks including a pilot.

### How much does route optimization cost?

Route optimization costs $20,000 to $50,000, depending on vehicles, time windows, driver hours, and other rules.

### Can you connect our carriers and warehouse?

Usually, yes. Each connection costs $8,000 to $25,000 depending on the partner's systems and how often data must move.

### Will the app work without signal?

Yes. Jobs, maps, and proof of delivery work offline and sync automatically when signal returns.

### Do we need custom software or an off-the-shelf product?

Off-the-shelf delivery products suit standard parcel work. Custom software makes sense when your process, partners, or pricing rules are unusual, or when per-driver fees grow faster than your business.

### What does logistics software cost to run?

Mapping and messaging services charge by usage, typically a few hundred dollars a month for a fleet of this size. Hosting is modest, and a care plan for business-critical software starts at $4,000 a month with a fast response for urgent issues.
