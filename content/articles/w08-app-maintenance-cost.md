Most owners budget carefully for the build and forget what comes next. Here is where the money actually goes after launch.

## Why apps need ongoing money

Software is not like a printed brochure. Everything around it keeps changing:

- Apple and Google release new operating systems every year
- Browsers and libraries update, and old versions become insecure
- Third-party services change their rules and prices
- Security threats evolve
- Users find problems and ask for improvements
- Your business changes

An app left alone for a year will usually start to break. After two years, it often needs a rebuild.

## The main cost areas

### 1. Hosting and infrastructure
Your app needs servers, databases and storage somewhere.

- Small apps: $20 to $150 a month
- Growing apps: $150 to $1,000 a month
- Large or high-traffic apps: $1,000 a month and up

Costs rise with users, data and traffic. Good setups scale up and down, so you don't pay for capacity you don't need.

### 2. Updates and compatibility
Every year, new iOS and Android versions arrive. Libraries and frameworks release updates. Someone has to test and update the app so it keeps working.

Expect several days to a few weeks of developer time each year, depending on how large the app is.

### 3. Bug fixes
Even well-tested apps have bugs. Real users find edge cases no tester imagined. A fix is cheap when done early and costly when ignored.

### 4. Security
Security is continuous, not a one-time check. It includes updating packages with known vulnerabilities, reviewing access, monitoring for unusual behavior and renewing certificates. For apps with payments or personal data, add periodic security reviews.

### 5. Third-party services
Many apps depend on paid services that bill by use:

- Email and SMS sending
- Push notifications
- Maps and location
- Payment processing fees
- Analytics and crash reporting
- AI and language model usage
- File storage and video

These are small at first and can grow quickly with success. Ask your developer for a list of every external service and its pricing model.

### 6. App store fees and rules
- Apple Developer Program: $99 a year
- Google Play: a one-time $25 registration fee
- Store commissions on in-app purchases, depending on program and rules

Stores also change their policies. Your app may need updates to stay listed.

### 7. Monitoring and support
Someone needs to watch for downtime and errors, and answer users. This can be a monitoring tool plus a developer on call, or a support plan with defined response times.

### 8. Improvements and new features
This is optional but it is where most of the value is. Apps that stop improving stop growing. Reserve a budget for new features, usually 10 to 30 percent of the build cost per year.

## What happens if you skip maintenance

**Short term:** nothing visible. That is why it is tempting to skip.

**After 6 to 12 months:** bugs pile up. New phones show layout problems. Some features stop working because a service changed.

**After 12 to 24 months:** security holes appear. The app may be removed from a store for not meeting new requirements. Updating becomes hard because many changes pile up at once.

**After 2 or more years:** a major rebuild is often cheaper than repairing it.

Regular small payments cost far less than one big emergency.

## How to control the cost

1. **Get a maintenance plan in writing before launch.** Know what is included, response times and the monthly or yearly price.
2. **Document everything.** Good documentation lets any competent team take over, which protects you from being locked in.
3. **Own your accounts.** Hosting, domains, app store accounts and code repositories should be in your name.
4. **Monitor usage costs.** Set billing alerts on cloud and third-party services.
5. **Reduce dependency sprawl.** Fewer third-party services mean fewer things to break and pay for.
6. **Review yearly.** Check what you pay for and what you no longer use.
7. **Choose boring technology.** Widely used tools have more developers, more support and lower long-term cost.

## Questions to ask your developer

- What will it cost to host this in year one, and how will it grow with users?
- What third-party services does it use, and how are they billed?
- What does your maintenance plan include, and what does it exclude?
- How quickly do you respond to a critical bug?
- How do you handle yearly operating system updates?
- If we stop working together, what do I receive to hand over to another team?

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to maintain an app per year?
Plan for 15 to 25 percent of the build cost per year for maintenance, plus hosting and service fees. Adding new features is extra.

### Do I really need a maintenance plan?
Yes, for any app that people depend on. Without it, updates, security fixes and bugs go unhandled until something breaks.

### What are the ongoing costs of a mobile app?
Hosting, maintenance, security, third-party services, app store fees, support and improvements.

### Can I maintain the app myself?
Only if you have a developer with the right skills. Most business owners are better off with a plan from the team that knows the code.

### How long can an app run without updates?
It might run for months, but problems grow quietly. After a year or two without updates, risks and repair costs rise sharply.

### Is it cheaper to rebuild than to maintain?
Rarely, in the first few years. Regular maintenance is cheaper than a rebuild. After many years, a rebuild may be the right choice.
